1. The Vietnam War

Unit 2 - Topic 1

Background

  • Vietnam had previously been ruled by France but had rebels like the Viet Minh, who were led by Ho Chi Minh.
  • 1945: Rebels declared independence from France and the Democratic Republic of Vietnam was created.
  • France resisted this rebellion but they withdrew in 1954. This meant that there was a power vacuum (no one was in charge) and this meant that the US were scared that Vietnam would turn to communism.
  • July 1954: Vietnam was split in two until elections were held to make a permanent government: the French left the North, and the Viet Minh left the South.

Western Involvement

  • Domino Theory: Worried that if South Vietnam (led by Diem) fell to communism it would spread to other neighbouring countries.
  • President Eisenhower’s New Look Policy: Sent money, equipment and military and political experts to South Vietnam (Diem) to help the country become secure from the threat of communism.

The Vietcong and Increasing US Involvement

  • In December 1960, the Vietcong (NLF) was formed to overthrow Diem.
  • The Ho Chi Minh trail was created to supply weapons to rebels from North Vietnam.
  • In 1960, US President John F. Kennedy sent 16,000 advisors to South Vietnam.
  • The US secretly supported the assassination of Diem on November 2, 1963, hoping for a better leader.
  • Kennedy was assassinated a month later. His successor, Lyndon B. Johnson, escalated US military involvement in Vietnam, leading to a war lasting over a decade with heavy casualties.

2. Space Race, Arms Race & The U2 Crisis

Unit 2 - Topic 2

Space Race

Reasons for: Propaganda (wanted to show they were superior) and weapons development (if they could launch rockets they could launch missiles and bomb countries).

4 October 1957: The USSR sends the first satellite to orbit Earth: Sputnik. Seen as a triumph for communism.
3 November 1957: The USSR sends the first animal into space, a dog named Laika.
12 April 1961: The USSR wins the race to send a human into space with cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin.
20 July 1969: Neil Armstrong becomes the first human being to walk on the moon.
15 July 1975: First joint space mission between the USA and the USSR: Apollo-Soyuz, symbolizing a new era of cooperation.

U2 Crisis (1960)

Events

U2 planes were spy planes flying at 23,000m trying to photograph weapons and learn about the Soviet Union. In May 1960, Gary Powers’ plane was shot down over the Soviet Union and he was captured, put on trial, and sentenced to 10 years in jail. Americans lied and claimed it was a weather plane rather than admitting to spying.

Impact

US government finally admitted it was a spy plane but Eisenhower refused to apologize. Khrushchev walked out of the Paris Peace Conference and called Eisenhower a "thief caught red-handed." Marked the end of Khrushchev's peaceful coexistence. Triggered increased fear of Russian spies in America (Joseph McCarthy’s anti-communist campaign and HUAC investigations).

The Arms Race & MAD

  • Nuclear Development: Soviets developed the Atomic bomb (29 August 1949). Americans developed the H-bomb in 1952; Soviets developed the H-bomb in 1953.
  • Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD): If one dropped the bomb the other would follow suit, effectively committing suicide. This deterred direct nuclear strikes.
  • Brinkmanship: Pushing a situation to the edge of nuclear war to force the other side to back down or make concessions.
  • Impact: Schools taught "duck and cover" drills; Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament (CND) organized widespread marches; popular culture produced anti-war films like Dr. Strangelove.

3. The Berlin Wall (1961)

Unit 2 - Topic 3
Background & Causes
  • Berlin divided since 1945. USSR saw West Berlin as an embarrassment; West saw it as a symbolic capitalist outpost in communist territory.
  • Many East Berliners defected to the West by crossing the border as they could travel freely. By 1961, thousands crossed daily, causing a severe "brain drain" that Khrushchev needed to stop.
Vienna Summit & The Wall
  • Vienna Summit (June 1961): Khrushchev dominated young, inexperienced JFK.
  • 13 August 1961: Border closed suddenly with barbed wire and fences to stop defectors.
  • October 1961 Tank Standoff: American diplomat denied entry, sparking a tense standoff where US and Soviet tanks faced off 5 meters apart before withdrawing.
  • June 1963: JFK visited West Berlin, delivering his famous "Ich bin ein Berliner" speech.
Consequences
  • Ended the refugee crisis by physically sealing East Berliners in.
  • Became the ultimate symbol of the Cold War division between East and West.
  • Permanently stabilized East Germany's regime while heightening global tension.

4. The Hungarian Crisis (1956)

Unit 2 - Topic 4
Background

Hungary was ruled by hardline Stalinist Mátyás Rákosi in 1949. His brutality and arrest of figures like Cardinal Mindszenty caused deep resentment. Hungarians demanded destalinization and flexibility in laws and policies.

Events & Escalation
  • 23 Oct 1956: Student protests demanded civil rights, Rákosi's removal, return of Imre Nagy, and Soviet troop withdrawal.
  • 31 Oct - 1 Nov: Khrushchev briefly withdrew troops; Nagy announced Hungary was neutral and leaving the Warsaw Pact.
  • 4 Nov 1956: Fearing a domino effect, Soviet tanks re-entered Budapest, crushing the uprising. Nagy was arrested and later executed, replaced by loyalist János Kádár.
Western Response & Impact
  • US containment policy did not trigger military intervention due to fear of direct conflict with Moscow. UN resolutions were easily vetoed by the USSR.
  • Showed Khrushchev was willing to use extreme violence despite talk of "peaceful coexistence."
  • Proved the USA would not interfere behind the Iron Curtain.

5. The Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)

Unit 2 - Topic 5

Background & Motivations

  • Soviet Retaliation: Khrushchev was anxious about U.S. nuclear missiles stationed in Turkey within strike range of the USSR, prompting him to place Soviet missiles in Cuba.
  • The Bay of Pigs (April 1961): A failed, U.S.-backed operation where trained Cuban exiles attempted to overthrow Fidel Castro. The humiliating failure drove Castro closer to the USSR.

The 13 Days Timeline (October 1962)

October 14–23:
  • Oct 14: U.S. spy planes spot missile sites in Cuba; JFK reveals it to the public.
  • Oct 21: JFK orders a naval quarantine of Cuba; US military placed on DEFCON 3.
  • Oct 24: DEFCON raised to DEFCON 2. Soviet ships approach quarantine line.
October 25–28 (Peak & Resolution):
  • Oct 27 (Black Saturday): Near-misses include a Soviet sub nearly firing a nuclear torpedo, a U-2 spy plane shot down, and a stray U.S. plane entering Soviet airspace.
  • Oct 28: Khrushchev agrees to remove missiles in exchange for a public U.S. pledge not to invade Cuba and a secret agreement to remove U.S. missiles from Turkey.

Long-Term Impact

  • Reduced Nuclear Brinkmanship: Pushed both superpowers toward arms control.
  • The Hotline: Established a direct communication link between Washington and Moscow to prevent accidental escalation.
  • Nuclear Test Ban Treaty (1963): Spurred early milestones in controlling nuclear weapons proliferation.

6. The Prague Spring & Czechoslovakia (1968)

Unit 2 - Topic 6
Background & Dubček's Reforms

By the 1960s, economic conditions worsened under unpopular leader Antonín Novotný. In January 1968, Alexander Dubček took over and introduced "socialism with a human face":

  • Removing state controls on industry for private businesses.
  • Ending press censorship and allowing free speech.
  • Granting Czechs the right to travel to non-communist countries.
  • Allowing independent trade unions.
Soviet Invasion & Brezhnev Doctrine
  • August 20, 1968: Warsaw Pact troops entered Czechoslovakia, seized Prague, and ended the Prague Spring. Around 100 protesters were killed.
  • The Brezhnev Doctrine (Nov 1968): Brezhnev declared that any Warsaw Pact country attempting reforms similar to Czechoslovakia would face immediate military intervention.
  • Western Reaction: Strongly condemned the invasion and cancelled a summit with Brezhnev, but took no direct military action due to détente and Vietnam commitments.

7. Détente & SALT 1

Unit 2 - Topic 7
Détente Definition

A period of improved relations, reduced hostility, and a general relaxing of tensions between the USA and the USSR during the 1970s.

Reasons for Détente
  • Sino-Soviet Split: US capitalized on the fallout between China and the USSR; Nixon visited China in February 1972.
  • Nuclear Threat: Fear of full-scale nuclear war after near-misses like the Cuban Missile Crisis.
  • Economic Strain: Both nations spent billions on arms; the USSR faced potential bankruptcy. US experienced war weariness from Vietnam.
SALT 1 (1972)

Strategic Arms Limitation Talks began in 1969, banning new ballistic missile deployments. Led to official treaties signed by Brezhnev and Nixon in May 1972, marking a major milestone in arms control.
