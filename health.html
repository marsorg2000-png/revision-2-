Factors Influencing British Medicine

Detailed Revision for Factors

GCSE exam questions routinely ask you to assess which factor was most significant in driving medical change or causing continuity. Master these 7 essential drivers across all four eras.

War

Forces rapid innovation in surgery and logistics. Examples: Paré treating gunshot wounds (1537), Nightingale in the Crimean War (1854), and WWI plastic surgery, X-rays & blood storage.

Science & Technology

Inventions uncover new truths. Examples: Gutenberg's Printing Press (1455), Leeuwenhoek's microscopes, Lister's carbolic spray, Röntgen's X-rays, ultrasound, and CT scanners.

Government & Politics

Shift from laissez-faire to state intervention. Examples: 1848 & 1875 Public Health Acts, 1911 National Insurance, Beveridge Report, and launching the NHS in 1948.

Religion & Beliefs

Both a brake and an engine of care. Examples: Church preserved Galen and banned dissection, yet monks provided clean monastery infirmaries and founded medieval hospitals.

Individual Genius

Pioneers who challenged existing dogmas. Examples: Vesalius, Harvey, Jenner, Snow, Pasteur, Koch, Simpson, Fleming, and Aneurin Bevan fighting for the NHS.

Chance & Luck

Accidental discoveries that altered medicine. Examples: Paré running out of boiling oil in 1537, and Fleming leaving a petri dish uncovered by a window in 1928 revealing penicillin.

Communication & Trade

Sharing medical treatises and global interactions. Examples: The Crusades bringing Islamic medicine to Europe, Renaissance anatomical illustrations, and royal medical journals.
