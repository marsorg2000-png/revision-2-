Comparing The Black Death (1348) & The Great Plague (1665)

Detailed breakdown of similarities (continuity) and differences (change) across causes, treatments, and public health action.

Category The Black Death (1348) • Medieval The Great Plague (1665) • Renaissance
Causes Believed (Continuity) • Punishment from God for widespread human sins.
• Planetary alignment & bad astrology.
• Bad air (miasma) from rotting dung and swamps.
• Scapegoating (falsely accusing Jews of poisoning water wells). 		• Still widely believed to be God’s punishment (days of prayer/fasting declared).
• Miasma (bad air) remained the dominant scientific explanation.
• Alignment of planets and heavenly comets blamed.
• Still zero understanding that fleas on rats carried bacteria.
Real Scientific Cause • Yersinia pestis bacterium living in the stomach of fleas (*Xenopsylla cheopis*) on black rats (*Rattus rattus*).
• Fleas bit infected rats, stomach blocked, regurgitated plague into humans.
• Population already malnourished from the Great Famine. 		• Identical biological pathogen: Yersinia pestis.
• Overcrowded wooden housing in London slums allowed massive black rat colonies to flourish.
• Warmer summer enabled fleas to breed rapidly.
Treatments & Remedies • Prayer, fasting, and extreme flagellation (whipping oneself).
• Unusual remedies: drinking mercury, crushed emeralds.
• Lancing painful buboes with heated knives.
• Carrying sweet herbs to counteract the bad air. 		• Bleeding with leeches to balance the humours.
• Smoking tobacco to drive away poisoned air (children punished if they refused!).
• Sniffing sponges soaked in vinegar.
• Strapping plucked live chickens or dried toads to buboes.
Public Health Action (Significant Change!) • Unorganised & ineffective: Local councils had no formal emergency system.
• Quarantine was virtually impossible to enforce in villages.
• Bodies left unburied or in shallow ditches; animals dug up corpses.
• Total collapse of municipal infrastructure. 		• Far more organised municipal response:
• Strict Quarantine: Infected homes locked from outside for 28 days with red crosses ("Lord have mercy upon us") and armed watchmen posted.
• Night Burial Pits: Dead collectors gathered corpses in carts after dark for mass deep burial pits.
• Fires in Streets: Large street bonfires burned sweet resins for 3 days to purify air.
• Banning Gatherings: Theatres, fairs, and alleyway congregations banned.
Learning Since 1348 • Very little understanding; people fled randomly, spreading the disease deeper into rural England. • Authorities noticed the connection between dirt and disease (even without knowing why).
• Trade was officially limited; certificates of health required.
• King Charles II and Court evacuated London to protect governance.
• Slaughter of 40,000 dogs and 200,000 cats (misguided, but demonstrated active state intervention).
Exam Writing Mastery

How to Answer the 8-Mark "Compare" Question on Medical Events

"Explain two ways in which responses to the Black Death (1348) were similar to responses to the Great Plague (1665)." OR "...how they were different." (8 marks)

1 Paragraph 1: Clear Point of Similarity

Formula: "One way in which responses were similar was the continuing belief that pestilence was caused by supernatural/miasmatic factors and the resulting religious treatments."

  • In 1348: People believed God sent the plague as punishment for sins; prayed, held religious processions, and took posies of herbs against bad air (miasma).
  • In 1665: People still believed it was divine wrath; observed national days of prayer/fasting, smoked tobacco and carried vinegar sponges against miasma.
  • Comparison link: In both events, lack of germ knowledge meant people relied on spiritual repentance and sweet-smelling air treatments.
2 Paragraph 2: Clear Point of Difference

Formula: "One way in which responses differed was that public health measures and state-enforced quarantine were far more organized in 1665 than in 1348."

  • In 1348: Councils were powerless; street cleaning failed, corpses were dug up from shallow pits, and quarantine broke down completely.
  • In 1665: London enacted official Plague Orders: infected houses boarded shut with red crosses and armed watchmen, bodies buried in night pits, gatherings banned, street fires maintained for 3 days.
  • Comparison link: While 1348 was chaotic and reactive, 1665 showed municipal governments actively attempting organized containment.
Top Examiner Tip for 8-Mark Questions: Always spend equal time on BOTH periods. Never write two separate essays; integrate direct comparison words: "Similarly...", "In contrast...", "Whereas in 1348..."
