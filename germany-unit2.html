1. Impact of the Great Depression on Germany

Topic 1

The Great Depression The American stock market collapsed in 1919 in the WALL STREET CRASH. This meant American companies were basically worthless and so the country went into an economic depression or downturn. Reasons for the impact on Germany:1. America recalled its loans as they were struggling for money themselves - they needed to repay more of their loans. 2. Germany lost part of their market - America used to buy a lot of German goods and now they had less money so were buying more.

Social Impact
  • Caused a reduction in confidence in the Weimar government – reminded them of the problems in the 20s.
  • Food prices were still high so people still couldn’t afford to buy enough food.
  • Due to unemployment people were forced to sell their possessions.
  • Wages fell by 1/3 in 1932.
  • Homelessness and squatting increased.
  • Crime increased and street gangs grew.
Political Impact
  • The welfare system could no longer cope with the needs of the country – had been set up for 800,000 unemployed not 6 million.
  • They cut unemployment benefits by 60%.
  • Supporters of different political parties fought on the streets and tried to disrupt each others meetings.
Economic Impact
  • No market for the goods produced → sales and profits fell.
  • 50,000 firms closed between 1929 and 1932.
  • Food prices were still high → no one could afford it → farmers making less profit → 1932: 18,000 farmers had gone bankrupt.
  • 1932 unemployment reached 6 million.

2. The Rise of the Nazi Party

Topic 2
The Role of Hitler & Mein Kampf
  • Got more publicity from the arrest after the Munich Putsch.
  • Nazi party struggled while Hitler was in prison.
  • He was a strong leader, persuasive, and an inspiring speaker.
  • Mein Kampf core ideas: Needed 1 strong leader; Treaty of Versailles should not be kept; government debate/discussion was negative; Aryans were superior; Communism was a threat; army needed rebuilding; all German-speaking people united.
Nazi Propaganda
  • Josef Goebbels put in charge of propaganda.
  • Made sure messages were clear, simple, and easy to understand.
  • The Nazis owned 120 daily or weekly newspapers read by hundreds of thousands, constantly putting across a positive message.
The SA (Stormtroopers)
  • Set up in 1921 to stop Nazi meetings being interrupted and disrupt communists.
  • Many members were unemployed and offered a way out of their situation.
  • Delivered propaganda directly to people’s homes.
  • Provided protection for those who felt threatened by communists.
Promises of the Nazi Party
✓ Designed to suit everyone
✓ Solve economic problems
✓ Increase food prices for farmers
✓ Support families
✓ Encourage good (conservative) morals
✓ Provide strong leadership
✓ Make Germany great again
Capitalizing on the Depression
  • People started listening to the policies and theories of the Nazis from the 1920s.
  • Blamed economic problems on Jewish people; people wanted someone to blame.
  • Framed the Treaty of Versailles as a crime while people were starving and losing jobs.
  • As unemployment rose, people looked to the Nazis in desperation and anger that nothing was being done.

3. Hitler’s Path to Dictatorship

Topic 3
1930: Election Success

Bruning was unpopular due to wanting to cut benefits. People turned to the Nazis in response, making them the 2nd largest party.

1930–1932: Emergency Powers

Bruning had no allies to pass laws, relying heavily on Hindenburg’s Article 48 emergency powers before being replaced by Von Papen.

July 1932: Election

Nazis won 37% of the vote, becoming the largest party. Hitler expected to be Chancellor, but it was given to unpopular Von Papen.

November 1932

Schleicher made Chancellor, but he also struggled to gain support or rule effectively.

End of 1932: Secret Deal

Hitler and Von Papen struck a secret deal for Chancellor and Vice-Chancellor positions.

January 1933: Hitler Chancellor

Papen convinced Hindenburg Hitler could be controlled, allowing only 3 Nazis in the initial cabinet.

27 Feb 1933: Reichstag Fire

Van der Lubbe (communist) blamed. 4,000 communist leaders arrested. Hindenburg granted Hitler emergency powers.

5 March 1933: New Election

Nazis secured 44% of the vote amidst intense police and SA voter intimidation.

24 March 1933: Enabling Act

Passed 444 to 94 (communists banned). Gave Hitler power to pass laws without Reichstag or President.

2 May 1933: Trade Unions Banned

Closed down and merged into the Nazi-controlled German Labour Front (DAF).

July 1933: One-Party State

All alternative political parties were officially banned in Germany.

2 Aug 1934: Death of Hindenburg

Hitler combined President and Chancellor roles to become **Fuhrer** (Supreme Leader), followed by Army Oath.

4. Causes of the Night of the Long Knives (June 1934)

Topic 4
1. Threat of the SA
  • Had grown to over 3 million members.
  • Being armed with weapons from abroad.
  • The SA were poorly disciplined, turning many citizens against the Nazi Party.
2. Rivalry Among Leaders
  • Goering and Himmler disliked Ernst Röhm’s massive influence.
  • Röhm opposed Hitler’s links to Prussian landowners and wanted a true socialist revolution.
  • The SS and Himmler wanted independence from SA control.
3. Role of the Regular Army
  • Army leaders feared Röhm and the SA taking over the regular military.
  • Hitler needed the regular army’s support—it was small, highly trained, disciplined, and possessed the power to overthrow him.
Outcome (June 29–30, 1934):

Hitler ordered the SS to arrest and execute Ernst Röhm and leading SA members. This eliminated internal rivals, secured the absolute loyalty of the regular German army, and cemented Hitler's supreme authority.
