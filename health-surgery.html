Key GCSE Revision Takeaway: Continuity vs Change

Amputations & Instruments (Continuity): Throughout Medieval, Renaissance, and early Industrial times, amputations and trephination remained common resort procedures. True transformation required scientific understanding of germ theory (Pasteur/Lister) and blood types/circulation.
The Anaesthetic ‘Black Period’ (Change): When anaesthetics (chloroform/ether) arrived in the 1840s, surgeons could operate deeper and longer, but because antiseptics had not yet been introduced, death rates initially climbed due to gangrene and sepsis until Lister's carbolic spray in 1867.
