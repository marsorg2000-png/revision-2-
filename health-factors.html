How to Tackle the 16-Mark Factor Question

"Has war been the most important factor in the development of medicine since c1000? Explain your answer with reference to war and other factors." (16 marks + 4 SPaG)

Named Factor Paragraphs

You must write in-depth about the factor named in the prompt across at least two distinct eras (e.g. Paré in 1537 and WWI Gillies / blood storage).

Counter-Factor Paragraphs

Evaluate at least two other factors (e.g. Government through the 1875 Public Health Act & 1948 NHS, or Science & Tech via Germ Theory & microscopes).

Sustained Judgement

Explain how factors interlink: e.g. War alone creates casualties, but without Government funding or Science & Tech (penicillin, X-rays), war cannot cure disease.

Factor Primary Role Best Turning-Point Evidence Key Limitation / Counter-Argument
War Accelerates surgical trauma treatments and logistics under crisis pressure. Paré’s 1537 wound ointment; WWI plastic surgery (Gillies) & sodium citrate blood storage; WWII mass penicillin. Disrupts long-term research, destroys universities/libraries, and only focuses on battlefield injury rather than disease prevention.
Religion Acted as an obstacle to anatomical dissection in the West, yet established compassion and preserved classical knowledge in Islamic hospitals. Church enforced Galen for 1,400 years; Islamic Baghdad hospitals (805) & Silk Road translations; monastic hospice care. Discouraged searching for natural causes; opposed human dissection and early anaesthetics (childbirth pain as God's will).
Government Provides legal mandates, nationwide finance, and state infrastructure. Crucial for public health. Compulsory 1875 Public Health Act; Bazalgette's £3m London sewers; Liberal reforms (1906–11); Bevan establishing the NHS (1948). Bound by laissez-faire policy before 1875; politicians reluctant to raise taxes on wealthy ratepayers without cholera crises.
Science & Tech Provides physical tools to see the invisible, disproving superstition and establishing evidence-based medicine. Gutenberg printing press (c1450); achromatic microscopes proving Germ Theory (1861); carbolic spray (1867); Coolidge X-rays (1913). Technology alone is useless without scientific method; early microscopes existed in 1670s but lacked magnification to prove microbes caused sickness.
Communication Spreads discoveries across international borders rapidly, preventing ideas dying with their discoverer. The Crusades sharing Islamic scholarship; Geminus translating Vesalius; Lister bringing Pasteur to Britain; Beveridge Report (630k copies). Without fast printing or literacy, communication in Medieval times took centuries through hand-copied monastic manuscripts.
Chance / Luck Accidental events that spark groundbreaking insights, but require prepared minds to recognise them. Paré running out of boiling oil (1537); Simpson spilling chloroform (1847); Fleming’s open window mould (1928); 1858 Great Stink. Chance alone creates nothing; Fleming could not purify penicillin; it required Florey, Chain, and US factory funding to save human lives.
Individual Genius Visionaries who had the courage to question established dogmas and champion empirical observation. Vesalius challenging Galen; Harvey's mechanical experiments; Jenner's cowpox trial; Snow's Broad Street pump map; Bevan's NHS determination. Individuals are constrained by their era's technology; Harvey could not see capillaries; Snow could not see the cholera bacterium.
